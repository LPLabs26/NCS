# Autonomous Instagram Team

## Purpose
Run NCS Aesthetics Instagram like a small high-performing marketing team without requiring Natalie to micromanage every task.

The system should:
- keep content moving every week
- surface only the decisions Natalie actually needs to make
- turn Natalie’s ideas into approved, scheduled posts
- maintain a premium, luxury, trust-building brand voice
- connect content output to inquiries, bookings, and retention

## Operating principle
Natalie is the owner and final approver, not the day-to-day content manager.

Her default role:
- drop in ideas, screenshots, promos, treatment notes, or client questions
- approve or reject posts that are ready
- answer only the small set of decisions that truly require owner judgment

The team handles everything else.

## Team roles

### 1. Strategist
Owns weekly direction.

Responsibilities:
- choose the weekly hero service or campaign
- translate business priorities into content priorities
- decide what mix of education, proof, offer, and retention content is needed
- review what performed last week and what should be repeated
- set the weekly scorecard targets

Outputs:
- weekly content brief
- weekly theme
- hero offer
- weekly priorities

### 2. Content Planner
Turns strategy into a production queue.

Responsibilities:
- collect ideas from Natalie, trends, FAQs, DMs, and prior wins
- assign each idea to a content pillar and funnel stage
- choose the best format: Reel, carousel, story, static, testimonial, or offer post
- keep the board updated from idea to posted
- make sure the week always has enough ready content

Outputs:
- prioritized content queue
- shot list requests
- caption assignments
- approval queue

### 3. Copy + Creative Producer
Builds post packages that are almost ready to publish.

Responsibilities:
- write hooks, captions, CTAs, on-screen text, and story frames
- package assets, before/after proof, talking points, and disclaimers
- keep language premium, clear, and conversion-oriented
- adapt one winning idea into multiple formats

Outputs:
- post-ready draft
- caption
- story set
- asset checklist

### 4. Approval Manager
Protects Natalie’s time.

Responsibilities:
- only send Natalie complete, easy-to-review approvals
- batch approvals instead of pinging her all day
- present simple options when a decision is needed
- capture approval status, edits, and turnaround time
- escalate only when blocked

Outputs:
- daily or batched approval queue
- approval notes
- revision requests
- approved-to-schedule list

### 5. Scheduler + Publisher
Gets approved content live.

Responsibilities:
- schedule approved posts
- ensure stories, feed posts, and offers match the calendar
- verify links, CTAs, and timing
- move published content into archive and performance review

Outputs:
- publishing calendar
- scheduled post log
- posted archive update

### 6. Community + Lead Manager
Turns attention into conversations and bookings.

Responsibilities:
- monitor comments, replies, DMs, and warm leads
- use approved responses and service recommendations
- flag objections or repeated client questions
- feed content opportunities back to the planner
- push rebooking and gap-fill opportunities when relevant

Outputs:
- DM follow-up queue
- FAQ / objection tracker
- lead status updates
- booking intent notes

### 7. Performance Analyst
Closes the loop every week.

Responsibilities:
- log what got reach, saves, replies, clicks, inquiries, and bookings
- identify winning hooks, services, and formats
- recommend what to repeat, cut, or test next
- quantify whether content is supporting bookings and retention

Outputs:
- weekly scorecard
- repeat / stop / test list
- insights for next week’s brief

## Owner-only decisions
Natalie should only be asked for:
- final post approval
- offer or pricing changes
- promo approvals
- sensitive brand or reputation calls
- claims, testimonials, or before/after content that feel legally or ethically sensitive
- campaign priority changes when the business goal shifts

## Everything the team should handle without Natalie
- drafting captions
- repackaging ideas into stronger hooks
- moving cards through the workflow
- requesting assets in a structured way
- batching approvals
- scheduling approved content
- pulling weekly metrics
- proposing next steps

## Success metrics
A good system produces:
- at least 1 week of content always in pipeline
- Natalie approval time under 15 minutes per batch
- no post stuck in approval longer than 48 hours without escalation
- daily story presence when business hours are active
- weekly review tied to bookings, not vanity metrics alone
