# Natalie Idea to Approval Flow

## Purpose
Make it easy for Natalie to contribute ideas without becoming the bottleneck.

## Natalie’s job
Natalie does not need to fully form a post.
She only needs to submit one of these:
- raw idea
- client question
- treatment insight
- screenshot
- promo thought
- availability update
- photo or clip worth using

That is enough for the team to work with.

## What happens after Natalie sends an idea
1. Idea is logged the same day
2. Planner tags it by service, pillar, and urgency
3. Producer turns it into a proper content package
4. Approval manager batches it back to Natalie when ready
5. Natalie approves, revises, or holds
6. Scheduler publishes it
7. Analyst logs performance and whether to reuse it

## Idea intake rules
When Natalie sends an idea, capture:
- date received
- exact wording if useful
- service connected to it
- why it matters now
- whether it feels urgent, seasonal, evergreen, or promo-driven

## If Natalie sends a vague note like:
- “we should talk about acne more”
- “I have openings Thursday”
- “clients keep asking about downtime”

The team should convert that into:
- a Reel or carousel concept
- a story sequence
- a caption angle
- a CTA linked to bookings or consults

## When the team can move without asking Natalie again
Proceed without another Natalie check when:
- the draft stays within approved voice and service positioning
- the offer itself is already approved
- the content is educational, FAQ-based, proof-based, or retention-based
- the only change is packaging, formatting, or scheduling

## When Natalie must be asked again
Ask Natalie if:
- the idea turns into a new promo or discount
- wording could create a risky treatment claim
- the post uses sensitive client proof
- the business priority has changed since the idea was submitted

## Fast approval format for Natalie
Each approval request should fit on one screen:
- Post: title
- Why now: reason
- Format: Reel/carousel/story/static
- Caption: final draft
- CTA: booking instruction
- Date: proposed publish date
- Decision: approve / revise / hold

## Success condition
Natalie feels like she is steering the system, not carrying it.
